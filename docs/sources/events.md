# Event Sources

References for event dates, categories, civilizations involved, and significance levels.

## Primary Chronologies

- Stearns (2001). Primary source for event dates and civilizations involved across all 105 events.
- Grun (2005). Cross-reference for year-level dating accuracy.

## Ancient and Classical Events

- Bickerman (1980). Date conventions for pre-Common Era events.
- Bauer, Susan Wise. *The History of the Ancient World: From the Earliest Accounts to the Fall of Rome*. New York: W. W. Norton, 2007.
  Bronze Age and Iron Age events: invention of writing, Battle of Kadesh, founding of Rome, Greco-Persian wars.

## Medieval and Early Modern Events

- Wickham, Chris. *The Inheritance of Rome: Illuminating the Dark Ages, 400--1000*. London: Penguin, 2009.
  Fall of Western Roman Empire, Rise of Islam, Viking expansion, Carolingian period.
- Abulafia, David. *The Great Sea: A Human History of the Mediterranean*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2011.
  Crusades, Fall of Constantinople, Mediterranean trade events.

## Modern Events

- Hobsbawm, Eric. *The Age of Revolution: 1789--1848*; *The Age of Capital: 1848--1875*; *The Age of Empire: 1875--1914*; *The Age of Extremes: 1914--1991*. London: Weidenfeld and Nicolson, 1962--1994.
  French Revolution through 20th-century events.

## 2.6.2 corrections

Events:

- **Bantu Migration** -500 -> -1500 (era Bronze Age). The expansion began
  c. 3000-1000 BCE, most often given as 2000-1500 BCE; by 500 BCE Bantu
  speakers had reached Lake Victoria. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion
- **Founding of the Ghana Empire** 830 -> 300, matching the Ghana Empire
  record; 830 is its first written mention (al-Khwarizmi).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire
- **Russian Revolution**: the February Revolution ended Tsarist rule; the
  Bolsheviks overthrew the Provisional Government in October.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution
- Civilization labels that contradicted the dates of the record they named:
  Bronze Age Collapse, Trojan War -> Mycenaean Civilization; Phoenician
  Alphabet -> Phoenicia only (the Greeks adopted it in the 8th century BCE);
  Rise of Islam -> Quraysh (the Caliphate begins 632); Founding of Tenochtitlan
  -> Mexica (the Aztec Empire begins 1428); Zagwe Dynasty -> Zagwe Dynasty (the
  Ethiopian Empire record begins 1270). First Crusade gains Kingdom of France.
- Descriptions corrected: Invention of the Compass (maritime use attested by
  1117), Abolition of the Atlantic Slave Trade (British participation only),
  Scramble for Africa (the Berlin Conference set rules; it did not partition),
  Human Genome Project (~92%; gapless in 2022).
  https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/researchers-generate-first-complete-gapless-sequence-human-genome
- Renamed: Treaty of Westphalia -> **Peace of Westphalia** (two treaties);
  Invention of the Smartphone -> **Launch of the iPhone** (the first smartphone
  was IBM's Simon, 1994). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Simon

Causal links:

- **Code of Hammurabi -> Founding of Rome** replaced by **Migration of the Sea
  Peoples -> Bronze Age Collapse**, a mainstream proposed cause.
- **French Revolution -> Invention of the World Wide Web** replaced by
  **French Revolution -> Haitian Revolution**.
- Strengths lowered to WEAK: Bronze Age Collapse -> Founding of Rome,
  Battle of Marathon -> Conquests of Alexander, Gutenberg -> World Wide Web.
- Rationales rewritten to match their nodes: Alexander -> Maurya (Chandragupta
  filled the vacuum, not Ashoka), Fall of the West -> Rise of Islam, Rise of
  Islam -> Mongol Conquests (the Otrar massacre of 1218).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otrar_Catastrophe

Interactions:

- Ancient Greece-Ancient Egypt ends -146 (with the Ancient Greece record),
  Viking-Byzantine ends 1066 (with Viking/Norse), Spain-Inca ends 1533 (with
  the Inca Empire record; Neo-Inca resistance to 1572 noted in the text).
- Egypt-Hittite war begins -1350, under Suppiluliuma I.
- Khmer-China is DIPLOMACY (tributary missions and trade); Khmer high culture
  was Indianized. Portugal-Kongo trade includes the slave trade.
